## Details

### First senior role and multi-project delivery

Resit was a software factory, and this was my first role with senior developer responsibilities. I worked on several client projects simultaneously as part of a cross-functional team. My work covered discovery, requirements, planning, solution design, and implementation, alongside guidance for other developers on architecture, tools, design patterns, and engineering practices.

I remained hands-on while helping the team plan and build these projects. I led architecture decisions for the projects and set engineering standards for TypeScript, React, and React Native. The client work covered government, culture, healthcare, industrial operations, legal services, and banking, so both the domain and the delivery environment changed between assignments.

### PIIA: Ministry of the Environment

One of my main projects was a platform for Chile's Ministry of the Environment. Institutions declared economic instruments with an environmental impact. The reporting goal was to present that information through reports and charts, including institution-level pollution indicators, to support decision-making.

The previous workflow relied on PDF documents. I was responsible for redesigning the functionality and developing a web application in which organizations could provide detailed responses. This involved turning an existing document-based process into an interactive application, not just reproducing the appearance of the PDFs.

I maintained direct, frequent communication with the client to gather requirements and understand how they conducted their surveys: the sequence of questions, dependencies between answers and later questions, and the different paths respondents needed to follow. The application also had to let users configure these question flows. Its form-building behavior was comparable to Google Forms, with more options for the ministry's particular questionnaires and conditional flows.

The ministry needed a custom application that it could host and control. Resit built it as an external supplier; this was not an internal ministry employment role. A generic hosted form service would not have met that delivery requirement.

The frontend used Angular 4 and JavaScript. The project also used Python, Django REST Framework, LoopBack, and MongoDB. React belonged to Escena, not to this project's frontend. The earlier source record also documents delivery under a short deadline and the project's role in opening further government opportunities for Resit.

### Escena: theatrical-production marketplace

Escena was a marketplace in a domain that was new to me. Theaters or producers could offer permission for other theaters to stage their plays. The product concerned the reuse of theatrical productions, rather than selling audience tickets to performances. I described that permission as a form of licensing; the exact legal arrangement was not part of the account.

I worked on the marketplace, its administrative panel, and payment-gateway integration. The application used React on the frontend and Node.js with Apollo and GraphQL for its API. GraphQL was attracting interest at the time, which was part of the reason we chose to use it.

My existing Resit record also includes payment integrations implemented through Express.js services and Transbank transaction flows for client projects. That experience belongs to the role; the specific payment provider for Escena has not been established separately.

### Construction-site access and attendance

Another project digitized access and attendance tracking at construction sites. Workers were accustomed to recording their attendance on dedicated time-clock hardware. We built an alternative around a tablet operating as a kiosk, where workers could identify themselves and record their attendance.

The solution included an administrative panel for reviewing access and attendance information, with analytics and charts. I worked on the application using React Native and Redux. The project brought mobile application development into an operational setting where the tablet was a shared workplace device.

### Eucerin: training and rewards

I created a training application for dermatologists associated with Eucerin. I built it with React Native. They received courses and challenges, earned points through those activities, and could redeem their points for rewards. The product combined training content with a points-and-rewards system.

### Legal document management

I also worked on an Angular application for legal document management. It supported handling and reviewing documents, opening cases, and working with the information associated with those cases. Document analysis and case workflows were central to the product.

### Banco de Chile outsourcing assignment

### Mentoring and engineering practices

I mentored five developers through pair programming, code reviews, and workshops. The workshops covered architecture, testing, and code quality. Mentoring was part of the day-to-day senior role, alongside implementing features and helping developers choose tools and design patterns.

### Introducing automated testing and CI/CD

As the team's Agile practices matured, I introduced and set up CI/CD at Resit. Before that, the company handled its delivery processes almost entirely manually. We already used GitLab, so I used it to establish pipelines that ran linting, tests, and formatting as part of continuous integration. I also set up the deployment process through continuous delivery.

The company did not have an established code-quality or automated-testing practice. I introduced unit testing for both existing code and new development, as well as end-to-end tests covering at least the critical user journeys. Our developments had strict code-coverage requirements intended to prevent defects and regressions. My responsibility included establishing these practices and the automation that ran the checks, rather than only contributing tests to an existing setup.

Adoption initially involved friction while these practices were still unfamiliar. We used workshops and pair programming to explain how they worked and help people apply them. These sessions supported the change in working practices as well as teaching the tools.

The team gained more confidence to deploy code much faster. On many occasions, unit tests and some end-to-end tests caught regressions and prevented changes from breaking existing functionality. These cases mainly involved mobile applications. This mattered in a fast-paced agency environment, where pressure to deliver quickly could compromise quality. The aim was to maintain code quality while working at that pace. These were observed team outcomes, not quantified changes in deployment speed or defect rates.

### Infrastructure and deployment environments

The infrastructure depended on each client's needs. I worked with conventional servers that required installation and configuration, as well as AWS and Google Cloud products for hosting applications, databases, and other services. The role therefore included server setup and cloud-based delivery rather than a single standardized hosting environment.
